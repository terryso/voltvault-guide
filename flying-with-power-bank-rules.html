Flying with a Power Bank: Airline Rules You Need to Know

You've probably wondered about this at least once. Here's the straight answer, no marketing fluff — just what actually matters and what you can ignore.

The 100Wh rule

Airlines limit spare batteries to 100Wh in carry-on without approval.

Watt-hours = (mAh × 3.7) / 1000. A 20000mAh bank is 74Wh — comfortably under the limit.

Carry-on only, always

Power banks are banned from checked luggage worldwide.

They must go in your carry-on or personal item — this is an IATA safety rule, not airline preference.

The 100–160Wh gray zone

Banks between 100–160Wh (roughly 27000–43000mAh) need airline approval, usually limited to two spares.

Above 160Wh is prohibited on passenger flights entirely.

What 20000mAh means in practice

At 74Wh, a 20000mAh bank flies on every airline with no paperwork.

It's the largest practical size that stays hassle-free — a big reason it's the most popular capacity.

At the security checkpoint

Keep the bank accessible — some airports ask you to place it in the tray separately, like a laptop.

A printed Wh rating on the bank speeds things up.
