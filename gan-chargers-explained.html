GaN Chargers Explained: Why Smaller Is Now More Powerful

You've probably wondered about this at least once. Here's the straight answer, no marketing fluff — just what actually matters and what you can ignore.

What GaN actually is

GaN (gallium nitride) is a semiconductor material that replaces silicon in charger circuits.

It switches faster and wastes less energy as heat, so the same power fits in a much smaller package.

Why size shrank

Less wasted heat means smaller heatsinks and tighter component layouts.

A 65W GaN charger is roughly half the size of its old silicon equivalent — that's why modern chargers feel impossibly small.

Does it charge faster?

GaN itself doesn't make charging faster — the wattage does.

What GaN gives you is 65W+ in a pocketable form. Check the wattage and PD support, not just the GaN label.

Cooler and more efficient

Higher efficiency means less heat on your desk or in your car.

In a hot vehicle this matters: cooler electronics last longer and throttle less.

Should you upgrade?

If your current charger is bulky, hot, or can't fast-charge your devices, yes.

If it already meets your wattage needs, GaN alone isn't a reason to replace a working charger.
