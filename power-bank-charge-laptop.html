Can a Power Bank Charge a Laptop? Wattage, Capacity & Real Expectations

You've probably wondered about this at least once. Here's the straight answer, no marketing fluff — just what actually matters and what you can ignore.

First, the wattage question

A laptop needs far more power than a phone. Most ultrabooks charge at 45–65W, larger laptops need 65–100W, and some gaming machines want 140W.

A typical phone power bank outputs 18–22.5W — that won't charge most laptops at all, or will only trickle while the laptop is asleep. The fix is USB-C PD (Power Delivery): the bank must list a PD output at or above your laptop's charger wattage. Check the wattage printed on your laptop's wall charger — that's the number to match.

How much charge will you actually get

A 20000mAh bank holds about 74Wh, but conversion losses eat roughly 15%, leaving around 60Wh of usable power. A MacBook Air battery is about 52Wh, so expect roughly one full charge.

A 14-inch Pro is around 70Wh, so expect about 80% of a charge. A 16-inch laptop is around 100Wh, so a 20000mAh bank gives you roughly half. If you need a full charge for a bigger machine, look at 27000mAh banks — they sit right at the 100Wh airline limit.

The cable matters as much as the bank

A 100W bank with a 60W cable only delivers 60W. USB-C cables have wattage ratings, and anything above 60W needs an e-marker chip inside the cable to negotiate the higher power.

Check the cable's rating before you blame the bank — and if a bank doesn't include a 100W cable, budget for one. A cable rated 240W is overkill today but won't hurt.

Charging while you work

A laptop draws power while you use it, so the bank's output and the laptop's draw are in a tug of war. Light work like browsing and documents draws roughly 10–20W, so a 65W bank charges the battery and powers the laptop at the same time.

Heavy work like video rendering or gaming can draw 60–90W, and then the battery may still drain — just more slowly. For the fastest top-up, close the lid or put the laptop to sleep while it charges.

What to look for when buying

Start with PD output: 45W covers most ultrabooks, 65W covers most 13–14 inch laptops, 100W covers larger Pro machines. Capacity of 20000mAh or more is the practical minimum — smaller banks run out before the laptop is full.

Expect 400–700 grams for a 20000–27000mAh bank with 65–100W output. Passthrough charging is worth having: the bank can charge itself while charging your laptop. And remember the flight rule: 100Wh max in carry-on, which is 27000mAh.

When a power bank isn't the answer

A power bank buys you hours, not a full workday.

For a gaming laptop with a 140W+ brick or a day of heavy rendering, no pocket-sized bank keeps up — a second wall charger at your destination is cheaper per watt-hour. But for a flight, a café afternoon or a commute with no outlet, a 65–100W bank is the difference between working and watching the battery icon.
