Are Solar Power Banks Worth It? Real Output vs Marketing

You've probably wondered about this at least once. Here's the straight answer, no marketing fluff — just what actually matters and what you can ignore.

What the panel actually delivers

Most solar power banks have a small panel rated around 5W, and cheaper ones as low as 1.5W. In direct midday sun a 5W panel realistically delivers 3–4W after conversion losses, and clouds or shade can cut that by 70–90%.

A full day in good sun yields roughly 15–20Wh — about one to one and a half phone charges. The panel is a trickle charger, not a wall outlet.

The math: how long to a full charge

A 10000mAh bank holds about 37Wh, and a 20000mAh bank about 74Wh. At a realistic 3W from the panel, refilling a 10000mAh bank from empty takes around 12 hours of direct sun — spread over two to three days of real-world use.

A 20000mAh bank doubles that. If you need a full bank by tomorrow morning, the wall charger is the only honest answer.

When solar actually makes sense

Solar earns its place on multi-day hikes, camping trips and emergency kits, where any charge beats no charge and you have hours of daylight to spare.

It also works as a maintenance trickle: leave the bank on your pack while walking and it slowly tops itself up. It makes little sense for daily commuting or city travel, where a wall outlet is always nearby.

What the marketing leaves out

Listings love phrases like 'charges in sunlight' without stating the panel wattage — if no wattage is listed, assume it's small.

Heat is another silent killer: a black bank baking in direct sun gets hot, and lithium cells charge slower and degrade faster when hot. And a panel only works while it's facing the sun; toss the bank in your bag and output drops to zero.

How to pick one, if you do

Look for a stated panel wattage of 5W or more — fold-out panels with 10W+ beat any built-in panel by a wide margin. Capacity of 10000–20000mAh is the sweet spot; bigger mostly means heavier.

Make sure it also charges fast from the wall via USB-C PD, because that's how you'll fill it 95% of the time. Check the weight and water resistance too: solar banks are usually heavier than a plain bank of the same capacity.
