Power Bank Not Charging? 7 Fixes to Try Before You Replace It

You've probably wondered about this at least once. Here's the straight answer, no marketing fluff — just what actually matters and what you can ignore.

Try a different cable first

Cables fail far more often than banks.

Swap in a known-good cable, preferably the one that came with your phone. Frayed or third-party cables often can't negotiate fast-charging protocols, so the bank falls back to a trickle or nothing.

Clean the ports

Pocket lint in USB-C ports is the #1 silent killer.

Power off the device, then gently pick lint out with a wooden toothpick. You'll be surprised how much comes out — and charging often works instantly after.

Check the input, not just the output

If the bank itself won't recharge, the problem is on the input side.

Use a wall adapter rated 18W or higher; a weak 5W phone charger can take 12+ hours to refill a 20000mAh bank, which feels like 'not charging'.

Wake a deeply discharged bank

Leave it plugged into a good charger for 30+ minutes even if no lights show.

Some protection circuits need sustained input before they'll wake up and start the indicator.

Rule out protocol mismatch

If charging starts then stops, the phone and bank may be failing the fast-charge handshake.

Try the bank's other port — many banks have one PD port and one standard port with different behavior.

Reset if your bank has a button

Hold the power button 10–15 seconds.

This resets the control chip on many models and clears false 'full' or 'empty' readings.

Know when it's actually dead

If the bank is 3+ years old, holds charge for half the time it used to, or gets hot while idle, the cells are worn out.

Lithium batteries degrade — replacement, not repair, is the answer.
